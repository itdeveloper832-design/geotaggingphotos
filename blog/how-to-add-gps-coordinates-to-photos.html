How to Add GPS Coordinates to Photos Online

calendar_today Published: August 2026 • schedule 8 min read

Adding GPS coordinates to photos helps photographers, real-estate professionals, travelers, drone pilots, and businesses organize their images and preserve accurate location information. Modern smartphones and cameras often save location data automatically, but there are many situations where you may need to add, edit, or replace GPS coordinates manually.

With GeoTaggingPhotos, you can upload one or multiple images, add latitude and longitude coordinates, inspect existing EXIF metadata, and download your updated photos directly from your browser—without installing any software.

In this guide, you'll learn how to geotag photos online in just a few simple steps.

pin_drop What Is Photo Geotagging?

Photo geotagging is the process of embedding geographical information, such as latitude, longitude, and altitude, into an image file. This location information is stored inside the image's EXIF metadata and can later be used to identify where the photo was captured.

Most smartphones automatically save GPS coordinates when location services are enabled. However, images transferred from cameras, drones, or editing software may lose this information, requiring users to add it manually.

Geotagging is commonly used by:

Photographers Real-estate agents Travelers Drone operators Construction teams Surveyors Journalists Researchers Tourism businesses

format_list_numbered Step-by-Step Guide to Geotag Photos Online

1

Upload Your Image or Images

Open the Add Geotag Tool and upload your photos using drag and drop or the file browser.

Supported formats include:

JPG JPEG PNG WebP TIFF HEIC

You can upload a single image or multiple photos at the same time. If you're editing several images captured at the same location, you only need to enter the coordinates once. The system automatically scans each uploaded file and checks whether GPS metadata already exists.

2

View Existing GPS Metadata

Many smartphones, drones, and cameras automatically save location information inside images. After uploading your photo, the tool automatically detects:

• Latitude & Longitude
• Altitude
• Capture date
• Camera model
• Device info
• EXIF metadata

If coordinates already exist, they will appear inside the editor. You can keep the existing location, replace it with a new one, or remove it completely. This feature is especially useful when updating old travel photos, organizing business assets, or correcting inaccurate locations.

3

Enter Latitude and Longitude Coordinates

Once your image has been uploaded, enter the coordinates you want to embed. You can:

  • Type coordinates manually.
  • Copy coordinates from Google Maps.
  • Paste existing GPS values.
  • Replace previous coordinates.
  • Edit altitude information.
Latitude: 40.7128
Longitude: -74.0060

If you're uploading multiple images from the same location, the coordinates will automatically be applied to every selected photo.

4

Use Your Current Location

Don't know the exact coordinates? No problem. Click the Use Current Location button, and your browser will request access to your device's GPS. Once permission is granted, the editor automatically fills the latitude and longitude fields.

Smartphones Tablets Laptops Desktops

Current-location detection is particularly useful for travel photography, real-estate listings, construction projects, drone inspections, and outdoor events. With a single click, you can apply your current location to one image or an entire batch of photos.

5

Add the Same Coordinates to Multiple Photos

Editing photos individually can take hours, especially when managing large image collections. With bulk geotagging, you can upload multiple images and apply the same location to every file simultaneously.

  • Upload your photos.
  • Enter the coordinates once.
  • Apply the location to all images.
  • Process the files.
  • Download the results.

    • Whether you're working with five images or five hundred, bulk editing saves time and eliminates repetitive work.
