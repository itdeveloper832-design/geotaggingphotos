How to Read EXIF Metadata from a Photo: Every Field Explained

calendar_today October 2026 • schedule 4 min read • verified_user 100% Client-Side Privacy
pin_drop

In This Guide

Hidden inside many photos is a complete record of how they were made: the camera, the lens, the exposure, the date, and sometimes the exact location. This guide explains every EXIF field in plain words, shows how to read them with the free browser viewer, and tells you what to check before you share a photo.

What EXIF Metadata Actually Contains

Every photo from a camera or phone can carry a hidden data block called EXIF metadata. It records how the picture was made and, when location was switched on, where it was made. The fields you will most often see are the camera make and model, the lens, the focal length, the aperture, the shutter speed, the ISO speed, the date taken, and the GPS coordinates. Together they tell the full story of a shot: which camera, which settings, when, and where.

How to Read EXIF on This Site

Open the free EXIF viewer and upload the photo. The viewer reads the metadata in your browser and lays out every field it finds: camera details, exposure settings, date, and GPS location with a map when coordinates exist. Nothing is uploaded to a server, so you can inspect private photos without sending them anywhere. If a field shows Unknown or N/A, the file simply does not store that value, which is normal for screenshots, exports, and edited copies.

What Each Field Means

A quick decoder for the fields the viewer shows:

  • Camera make and model. Which camera or phone took the photo.
  • Lens and focal length. Which lens was used and how zoomed-in the shot is; a longer focal length means a tighter, more magnified view.
  • Aperture. How wide the lens opened. A wide aperture lets in more light and blurs the background; a narrow one keeps more of the scene sharp.
  • Shutter speed. How long the sensor was exposed. Fast speeds freeze motion; slow speeds blur it.
  • ISO speed. How sensitive the sensor was. Higher ISO brightens dark scenes but adds grain.
  • Date taken. When the shutter fired, as recorded by the camera clock.
  • GPS coordinates. Where the camera was, as latitude and longitude, when location was on.

EXIF vs GPS: What Is the Difference?

GPS is one part of EXIF, not a separate thing. A photo can carry full camera data with no GPS at all, and it can carry GPS with almost no camera data. That is why the two checks on this site exist side by side: the photo location finder answers only the where question, while the EXIF viewer answers everything. If you only care about location, use the finder. If you want the complete picture, use the viewer. Our guide on how to check if a photo has GPS coordinates covers the GPS-only check in detail.

When EXIF Is Missing or Incomplete

Missing fields are not an error. Screenshots carry no camera EXIF at all. Photos sent through messaging apps or social platforms often arrive stripped. Scanned prints never had digital metadata. Edited and re-exported copies may keep some fields and lose others. When the viewer shows Unknown or N/A, trust it: that value is genuinely absent from the file, and no reader can recover what was never stored or was stripped away.

Privacy: What to Check Before Sharing

Before you post a photo publicly, run it through the viewer and look at two things first: the GPS fields and the date. Those two together can reveal where you were and when, which is enough to reconstruct routines. If either worries you, share a copy without that metadata instead, and verify the copy in the viewer again before posting. Our EXIF removal guide walks through the full check-remove-verify routine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all photos have EXIF data?

No. Photos from cameras and phones usually do, but screenshots, scans, and photos stripped by messaging or social apps may carry little or nothing. The viewer shows exactly what is present.

Why do some fields show Unknown or N/A?

Because the file does not store those values. That is normal for screenshots, exports, and edited copies, and it is not a fault of the viewer.

Does EXIF survive WhatsApp or social media uploads?

Often it does not: many platforms strip metadata when you upload. Do not rely on that, though. Verify the exact file you share instead of trusting the platform.

Can I edit EXIF data with the viewer?

The viewer only reads. To set or replace GPS coordinates, use the add geotag tool. For wider EXIF editing, use a dedicated metadata editor on your own device.

Is EXIF the same as the file properties my computer shows?

Not quite. Your operating system shows a subset of the same data. The viewer reads the full EXIF block, including GPS and camera fields the file properties panel may hide.
