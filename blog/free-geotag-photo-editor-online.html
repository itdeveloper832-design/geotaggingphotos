What a Geotag Photo Editor Does

A geotag photo editor is a tool that changes the location stored inside a photo. Every photo can carry hidden EXIF metadata, and the GPS section of that metadata holds the latitude and longitude where the picture was taken. An editor lets you view those coordinates, set new ones, or replace a wrong location, without changing a single pixel of the image itself.

That last point matters. Editing a geotag is metadata work, not image editing. Your photo looks exactly the same afterwards. Only the hidden data block changes, which is why a good editor can work entirely in your browser with no quality loss.

What You Can Edit

With a browser-based geotag editor you can:

  • Add GPS coordinates to a photo that has no location at all.
  • Replace a wrong location, for example when a phone recorded a nearby place instead of the exact spot.
  • Fix a whole batch by applying one set of coordinates to many photos at once.
  • Check your result by reading the updated EXIF data back and seeing the location on a map.

The GeoTaggingPhotos editor handles latitude, longitude, and EXIF metadata, and you set the position either by dropping a pin on an interactive map or by typing the coordinates directly.

Why a Browser Editor Instead of Desktop Software

Desktop photo tools can edit metadata too, but they need a download, an install, and sometimes a paid licence. A browser editor needs none of that. You open a page, edit, and download. There is also a privacy difference: because everything runs on your device, your photos are never uploaded to a server. For a quick location fix, the browser route is simply faster.

Step by Step: Edit a Photo's GPS Location

Open the add geotag tool and upload your photo. You will see the photo and a map side by side. Either drag the pin to the exact place or type the latitude and longitude into the coordinate fields. When the position looks right, process the photo and download it. That downloaded file is your edited photo; the original on your device is left untouched, so you can always compare the two.

Editing Many Photos at Once

One location often belongs to a whole set of photos: a wedding venue, a property listing, a day trip. Editing each file by hand would take forever. The bulk geotagging tool applies the same coordinates to an entire batch and bundles the results into a single ZIP download. It is the same private, in-browser process, just scaled up.

Checking Your Edit Actually Worked

Never trust an edit you have not verified. Open the downloaded photo in the free EXIF viewer and confirm the GPS coordinates match what you set, then check the spot on the map. If you want to find where an existing photo was taken, the photo location finder reads the embedded coordinates and shows the capture location.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Three mistakes cause most failed edits. First, swapped coordinates: latitude always comes first, and it ranges from -90 to 90, while longitude ranges from -180 to 180. Second, the wrong hemisphere: a missing minus sign can move your photo to the other side of the planet. Third, forgetting to download: edits live in the processed file, so always download the result and check it in the viewer before you share it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this geotag photo editor really free?

Yes. The browser editor is free to use, with no account, no install, and no watermark on your photos.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. All editing happens in your browser on your own device. Your photos never leave your computer or phone.

Does editing GPS data change image quality?

No. GPS and EXIF edits only change the hidden metadata block. The image pixels are untouched, so quality stays exactly the same.

Can I remove a geotag with this editor?

The editor sets and replaces coordinates. To see what location a photo currently stores, check it first with the free EXIF viewer, then set the correct coordinates with the editor.

Which photo format works best for GPS data?

JPEG (.jpg) is the standard format for EXIF GPS data, and it is the most reliable choice when you want a location to stick with a photo.
