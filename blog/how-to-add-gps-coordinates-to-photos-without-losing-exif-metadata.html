How to Add GPS Coordinates to Photos Without Losing EXIF

calendar_today Published: August 2026 • schedule 8 min read

Adding GPS coordinates to photos is a simple way to preserve where an image was captured, organize large photo collections, and maintain useful location information inside image metadata. Modern smartphones often record location automatically, but GPS information can disappear when photos are edited, exported, converted, compressed, or shared through certain platforms.

This complete guide explains how photo GPS coordinates work, where they are stored, how to check existing location metadata, how to add or replace GPS information, and how to preserve important EXIF metadata when processing your images. It also covers bulk geotagging, current-location detection, GPS privacy, SEO considerations, and common mistakes to avoid.

pin_drop What Are GPS Coordinates in a Photo?

GPS coordinates in a photo are geographical values that identify where an image was captured or assigned a location. The two primary values are latitude and longitude. Some images also contain altitude, GPS time, direction, and other location-related information.

When GPS information is stored inside an image's metadata, the photo can carry its location with it even when the image is moved between devices or stored in a digital photo library.

explore What Do Latitude and Longitude Mean?

Latitude identifies a position north or south of the Equator, while longitude identifies a position east or west of the Prime Meridian. Together, these values can identify a location anywhere on Earth. For example, coordinates 40.7128, -74.0060 represent New York City.

photo_camera What Is GPS EXIF Metadata?

EXIF stands for Exchangeable Image File Format. It is a metadata standard used by many image formats to store information about photographs, camera settings (lens, shutter speed, ISO, capture date), and embedded GPS coordinates.

folder_zip Where Are GPS Coordinates Stored in a Photo?

GPS location information is commonly stored inside the EXIF metadata of supported image files. The actual image pixels and the metadata are separate types of information within the file, which means metadata can sometimes be changed without visibly changing the photograph.

Common GPS EXIF Fields Header Tags:

GPSLatitude
GPSLongitude
GPSLatitudeRef
GPSLongitudeRef
GPSAltitude
GPSTimeStamp

Note: A photo can contain extensive EXIF metadata without containing any GPS information if location services or camera permissions were disabled at the time of capture.

warning Why Are GPS Coordinates Sometimes Missing From Photos?

location_off Location Services Disabled

If location services were disabled during capture, the camera could not access GPS signals to record position.

lock Camera Permission Denied

Smartphones require explicit app permissions. If location access was denied, new photos won't have GPS tags.

edit_off Editing Software Stripped Data

Export workflows in editing tools often strip metadata by default during compression or save-for-web operations.

crop_original Screenshots & Conversions

Screenshots create brand-new image files and do not preserve original photo EXIF headers.

visibility How to Check Whether a Photo Already Has GPS Coordinates

Before adding new GPS information, check whether your photo already contains location metadata. This prevents accidentally replacing accurate coordinates with incorrect information. Use our online EXIF Viewer to inspect available camera metadata, exposure details, and GPS tags before editing.

Windows PC

Right-click image file > Properties > Details tab > Scroll down to GPS section.

macOS Finder

Select photo in Finder or Preview > Press Cmd+I (Get Info) or Inspector > View EXIF GPS tab.

travel_explore Does Geotagging Photos Improve Google SEO?

Geotagging and SEO are frequently discussed together, particularly in local SEO and real-estate photography. However, it is important to separate established search-engine practices from claims that GPS metadata is a guaranteed ranking factor.

check_circle What GPS Metadata Helps With

Organizing digital assets, field documentation, mapping projects, and internal location management.

cancel What GPS Metadata Is Not

It is not a secret Google ranking silver bullet. Adding coordinates does not guarantee instant Google Images ranking.

Better ways to optimize images for search include descriptive alt text, proper filenames, responsive images, structured data, and high-quality surrounding page content.

shield_lock GPS Metadata Privacy & Social Media Platform Stripping

Many social media and messaging platforms strip or re-encode EXIF metadata upon upload to protect user privacy and reduce file bandwidth. If preserving GPS headers is essential for professional delivery, share raw files or ZIP archives directly.

privacy_tip When to Remove GPS Metadata for Safety:

  • Photos taken inside or near private residences
  • Photos involving children or personal family events
  • Confidential commercial or sensitive defense sites

gpp_bad Common Photo Geotagging Mistakes to Avoid

1. Swapping Lat & Lng

Reversing latitude and longitude values places your photo in completely wrong oceans or continents.

2. Omitting Minus Signs

Western hemisphere longitudes and Southern latitudes require negative values (e.g. -74.0060).

3. Wrong Batch Coordinates

Double check location parameters before running bulk processing on hundreds of photos.

4. Assuming Metadata Survives

Always test exported files after saving in external graphics apps to verify EXIF headers remain.

5. Exposing Home Locations

Remove residential coordinates before publishing public portfolio images online.

6. No Backup Originals

Keep unedited master copies of important photographs before modifying metadata.
